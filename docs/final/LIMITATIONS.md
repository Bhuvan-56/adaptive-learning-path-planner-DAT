# Limitations (final, Week 8)

What the project does **not** show, and known defects that are still open at the end of Week 8. Each item names where the evidence is. Fixed items are listed at the end so the history is complete.

## A. What the evidence can and cannot support

1. **Simulated learners only.** Every planning outcome comes from a simulator, and no real learner used the planner (scope: `docs/PROBLEM_BOUNDARY.md`). No claim about real learning gains is made. Public data is used in one place only: BKT fitted on ASSISTments 2009–2010 students and tested on held-out students, and the S4 scenario, whose simulated learners take their parameters from those fits (`docs/w8/results_assistments.md`).
2. **The simulator matches the planner's own model.** S0 and S1 use a BKT generator with the same `p_S`, `p_G` and `p_T` as the learner-state model (`src/evaluation/integrated.py`, `make_learner_generator`). The planner is therefore tested on learners that behave exactly as it assumes, which is the most favourable case. Week 8 (addendum 2) also ran the mismatched PFA generator (S3), and the adaptive advantage held there (`docs/w8/results_maths.md`). But S3 rewards the number of practice attempts directly, which favours the adaptive arm's extra practice, so S3 is a different simulator, not a neutral one. S4 (Week 8 public-data extension) gives each learner per-concept slip, guess and learn values drawn from real-student fits, while the engine keeps its defaults. The adaptive advantage held there too (adaptive − static +0.0550 [0.0270, 0.0830]), but only on the toy curriculum without forgetting.
3. **Curriculum size.** Most integrated results use the 4-concept toy curriculum (5 days, 10 minutes a day). Week 8 added runs on the 16-concept maths curriculum (10 days, 30 minutes a day; `docs/w8/results_maths.md`), where best-effort plans are used. Remediation still never changes a schedule (B1, B2); since the Week 8 final fixes the diagnostic and review do (B3, B12). Both curricula are hand-made; neither comes from a real course or dataset. ASSISTments has no prerequisite fields, so no edges were taken from it.
4. **S2 (difficulty-sensitive) is not verified.** It was not rerun after the Week 7 and Week 8 fixes. Week 6 S2 tables show S0 figures; whether difficulty sensitivity reaches the outcome was never checked (`weekly/W7.md`, correction to the Week 6 record).
5. **σ² is a proxy.** `σ² = p(1−p)/(κ+1)` counts evidence. It is not a calibrated Bayesian credible interval (`decisions/ADR-005-uncertainty-representation.md`).
6. **HLR half-life is a fixed prior** (14 days, `experiments/m8_learner_eval.yaml`, read by `src/planning/review.py`), not fitted to data.
7. **Runtimes** come from one Windows laptop and one run per configuration. The S4 run's runtimes are about 4× those of S0 because of machine load and are not compared.
8. **The engine keeps its default BKT parameters** (`p_S` 0.10, `p_G` 0.25, `p_T` 0.10), although parameters fitted on ASSISTments predict held-out real students better: log loss −0.0288 [−0.0378, −0.0192] per student (ADR-023). Under S4 this costs calibration: adaptive_full's mastery Brier is 0.1607, against 0.1358 under S0. The fitted skills are ASSISTments skills, not the project's concepts, and a few fits are poorly identified (guess up to 0.72). S4 excludes the 3 skills with guess or slip ≥ 0.5.
